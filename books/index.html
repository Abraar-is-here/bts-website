The books

Every division head runs a paper-trading book at Interactive Brokers with their analysts. The figures here come straight from IBKR’s own statements: returns, risk, positions and every trade, worked out the way a fund reports them.

Sample data: invented trades that show the format. Not real results.

Loading the books

How the numbers are worked out

Each head exports their account’s statement from Interactive Brokers (a Flex Query) and it is published here unchanged. Every figure on this page is then calculated in your browser from that file, by the rules below, so anyone can check them.

Paper trading: simulated accounts with no real money, so there is no slippage or market impact beyond what IBKR’s simulator applies. Prices are IBKR’s closing marks, not live. Nothing here is investment advice.

Return since inception
Each day’s change in net asset value (NAV), less any deposit that day, divided by the day before’s NAV, then chained: (1 + r₁)(1 + r₂)… − 1. A time-weighted return, so topping up an account never counts as performance.
Profit and loss
The change in NAV over the period, less deposits, in the book’s base currency. It is after IBKR’s commissions.
Annualised return
The return scaled to a year of 252 trading days. Shown once a book has six months of history; before that it exaggerates.
Best day, worst day, days up
The largest one-day gain and loss, and the share of trading days that ended higher than they started.
Sharpe ratio
Average daily return divided by the standard deviation of daily returns, times √252. The risk-free rate is taken as zero. Shown after 20 trading days: fewer than that and it is mostly noise.
Sortino ratio
Like Sharpe, but only falls count as risk: average daily return over the downside deviation, times √252.
Volatility
The standard deviation of daily returns, annualised by √252.
Drawdown
How far the book is below the highest value it has reached. Max drawdown is the deepest fall from a high; the longest drawdown counts trading days spent below a previous high.
Closed trades and fills
A trade runs from the fill that opens a position to the fill that takes it back to zero, however many fills there are in between. A fill is one execution at IBKR.
Win rate
The share of closed trades that made money after commissions.
Profit factor
Total profit on winning trades divided by total loss on losing ones. Above 1 means the book makes money on its closed trades.
Expectancy
The average profit or loss per closed trade: what a typical trade has been worth.
Average win and loss
The average profit on winning trades and loss on losing ones. Read with the win rate: a low win rate can still pay if the wins are much larger.
Average hold
Calendar days from a trade’s first fill to its last.
Exposure
Position value as a share of capital, with futures at their full contract value. Gross adds longs and shorts together; net subtracts the shorts.
Trade P&L
IBKR’s first-in, first-out realised P&L, which includes commissions on both legs. IBKR gives none for currency conversions, so for FX it is worked out here the same way.
