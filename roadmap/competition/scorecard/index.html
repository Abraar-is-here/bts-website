Bristol Trading Competition 2026

Pitch scorecard

Each desk pitches one trade idea in four minutes, then takes two to three minutes of questions. Mark every desk on the same six criteria, from 1 to 10 each, for a total out of 60.

When
Mon 30 Nov, 6 pm
Where
Pugsley Lecture Theatre, Queens Building
Each pitch
4 minutes, cut off at 4:00, then questions

How to mark

  1. Mark each desk straight after its questions, before the next desk starts. Write a word or two of notes; they help when two desks end up close.
  2. Use whole numbers from 1 to 10. A 5 is a sound pitch with nothing wrong and nothing special. Keep 9 and 10 for a trade you would put your own money behind.
  3. Mark on your own and don’t compare marks with the other judges until every desk has pitched.
  4. Judge the idea and how it is argued, not whether you agree with it. A well-argued short of something you like can still score 10.
  5. You won’t be told how any desk did in the trading week, and it shouldn’t affect your marks.

The criteria

Every pitch has to cover three parts in order: the trade, the thesis, then catalysts and risks. The first five criteria are the ones the desks were briefed on; the sixth covers the questions.

The six criteria, and what a pitch at each band of marks looks like
Criterion1–34–78–10
Clarity of thesisout of 10Could you say what they are trading, and why, after the first minute? It is unclear what they are trading or why. Or the view is the same as the market’s, so there is no trade. The trade is clear by the end of the first minute. What the market is pricing, and why the desk disagrees, is vague or only implied. The trade is said in one sentence in the first 20 seconds. The desk says plainly what the market prices and why it is wrong.
Analysis and evidenceout of 10Do the data and reasoning support the view? Claims without evidence, or numbers that don’t support the view. Sensible drivers, but the support is thin or generic, or rests on assumptions they don’t test. One to three drivers, each backed by data or analysis that bears on the price. Few, strong reasons rather than a long list.
Trade constructionout of 10Are entry, target and stop sensible, and is the risk worth taking? Required fields are missing, there is no stop, or the levels don’t add up. All six fields are there, but the levels look arbitrary or the risk/reward is marginal. Instrument, direction, entry, target, stop, risk/reward and horizon are all given early. The levels follow from the thesis and the horizon fits the desk’s range.
Catalysts and risksout of 10Do they know what moves it, when, and what would prove them wrong? No catalysts. Risks skipped or mentioned in passing. Catalysts are named but not dated, or generic (“upcoming earnings”). Risks are listed without saying what would end the trade. Dated catalysts inside the horizon, each with what it should show. The main risks are faced honestly, and the level that proves them wrong matches the stop.
Delivery and energyout of 10Would you listen to them pitch it again? Hard to follow, read off the slides, or cut off at 4:00 before the main points. Clear but flat or rushed. Leans on the slides in places. Confident and engaging. Talks to the panel, not the screen, and finishes inside four minutes.
Questionsout of 10How do they handle your questions, and how good are theirs? Dodges questions or can’t answer them. Asks nothing of other desks. Relevant but general answers. Few questions of other desks, or soft ones. Answers directly, with numbers, and knows where the trade is weak. Asks other desks sharp questions about the thesis or the risk.
