03

Phase 2: the pitch

Each desk presents a trade idea to a panel of external judges.

4 minutes to pitch. You will be cut off at 4:00, whether or not you have finished your slides. Then 2–3 minutes of questions from the judges.

The trade

  • It must be a real, tradable instrument. It does not have to be on IBKR.
  • Long, short, futures and options are all allowed.

Time horizon

DeskGroupHorizon
Equities desksEquities1 week to 12 months
Fixed Income, FX, Oil and MetalsFICC1 week to 3 months
Macro and QuantThe asset you pitchThe range for that asset’s group

What every pitch must cover

Every pitch has the same three parts below. How you design the slides is up to you, but the order isn’t.

Part 1The trade

Open with the trade. Say it in one sentence within the first 20 seconds: what you are trading, which way, and for how long.

“We are long the Nikkei 225 for three months.”

Then show the trade summary early, ideally on slide 1 or 2. These six are required from every desk:

FieldWhat to give
Instrument and directionThe exact instrument (ticker, contract or bond) and whether you are long or short
EntryThe price or level you would enter at
TargetThe price or level where you take profit
Stop lossThe price or level where you accept you are wrong and exit
Risk/rewardDistance to target divided by distance to stop, e.g. 2.5 : 1
Time horizonHow long you expect to hold, within your group’s range above

You can add more, such as position size or conviction, but these six must be there.

Part 2The thesis

Your thesis should cover two things:

  • Consensus vs our view. What the market is pricing today, and why you think it is wrong. This is the heart of the pitch. If you agree with the market, there is no trade.
  • 1 to 3 key drivers. The reasons you think the price will move your way, each supported by data or analysis. Fewer, stronger drivers beat a long list.

Part 3Catalysts and risks

  • Dated catalysts. The specific events within your horizon that should move the price, each with its date and what you expect it to show. “Q4 earnings on 28 January”, not “upcoming earnings”.
  • Top risks and invalidation. The two or three things most likely to go wrong, and the price level or event that would prove you wrong. This is the logic behind your stop loss.

Slides

  • There is no slide limit. Time is the limit: choose how many slides, and what to cover, so that you finish inside 4 minutes.
  • Graphs, charts, tables and other ways of showing data are welcome.
04

How the judges score the pitch

The judges score every desk on the same five criteria.

CriterionWhat the judges look for
Clarity of thesisThe trade and the reason for it are clear within the first minute, and the view differs from consensus
Analysis and evidenceThe data and reasoning genuinely support the view
Trade constructionEntry, target and stop are sensible, with a risk/reward that justifies the trade
Catalysts and risksCatalysts are specific and dated; honest about what could go wrong and where the trade is invalidated
Delivery and energyConfident, engaging and on time
05

Questions: the extra 10%

Points on the day go to desks that:

  • Ask sharp, relevant questions of other desks after their pitches; and
  • Answer the judges’ questions on their own pitch clearly and directly.

A good question tests the thesis or the risk, not the formatting. Prepare your analysts to listen to other pitches and ask.

06

What to fix from the bi-weekly meeting

  • Time. Several desks ran over their 5 minutes. This time the cut-off at 4:00 is absolute.
  • Energy. Pitch it like you believe it. Stand up straight, look at the judges, open strongly and do not read from the slides. You are selling a trade, not a funeral.
  • Clarity. After 30 seconds, the judges should be able to say what you are trading and why. If they cannot, start again with the one-sentence trade.
