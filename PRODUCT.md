# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

- **Prospective members / analysts** — Bristol students deciding whether to apply to the analyst programme (40–50 selected/year) or a division head role. They're comparing BTS against other university societies and want proof the experience is real, not just social.
- **Sponsors and industry partners** — firms (DRW, eToro, Standard Chartered, etc.) deciding whether a partnership is worth their time. They're judging credibility and reach (800 members, 4 divisions) before committing.
- **Speakers and external guests** — industry professionals invited to speak or judge; the site is often the first thing they see about the society.
- **Alumni and committee** — checking their own bios/photos, and using the site as the public face of decisions made internally.

The site is brand-led (design IS the product — it has to look like a serious finance operation, not a student club page), but it carries real operational weight: division head application deadlines, event dates, and sponsor asks are read literally and acted on. Copy accuracy and currency matter as much as visual polish.

## Product Purpose

Convince three audiences at once — applicants, sponsors, speakers — that BTS is a credible, competitive, high-production-value trading society at the University of Bristol, while also functioning as the operational source of truth for recruitment cycles (division head and analyst applications) and the events calendar. Success looks like: strong applicant volume for competitive roles, sponsors renewing/expanding partnerships, and no one showing up to the wrong date because the site was stale.

## Brand Personality

Credible & institutional, ambitious & competitive, polished & approachable. It should read as a serious finance operation (the kind a sponsor takes seriously) without tipping into corporate stiffness — still a student society, still warm enough that a nervous first-year applicant doesn't feel shut out.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate bank site** — no jargon-heavy institutional stiffness, no faceless legal-department tone. Avoid feeling like a big bank's investor-relations page.
- (Implicit, from existing site conventions) generic templated university-society pages — flat card grids, no real photography, no evidence of actual output.

## Design Principles

1. **Prove it, don't claim it** — real competition photos, real speaker names/roles, real numbers (800 members, 40–50 analysts, 4 divisions) over generic marketing language.
2. **Credible first, ambitious second** — institutional trust (for sponsors) is the floor; competitive/ambitious energy is the differentiator on top of it, not a replacement for it.
3. **Warm enough to apply to** — a first-year deciding whether to apply should feel invited, not intimidated; approachability tempers the institutional tone.
4. **Operational accuracy is part of the design** — deadlines, dates, and event details are load-bearing content, not filler; stale or wrong info is a worse failure than a visual rough edge.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific formal requirement was set. Default to standard WCAG AA practice (color contrast, keyboard navigation, `prefers-reduced-motion` support) as sane baseline hygiene, without treating it as a hard project constraint.
